The `/order` slash command lets you send an item to a teammate directly from Slack. Run the command in any channel or DM where the Archer app is installed, fill in a short modal, and Archer queues the order for fulfillment.

Use this when you want to reward a teammate in the moment — for example, sending a gift or piece of swag — without leaving Slack to open the Archer dashboard.

## Prerequisites

- The Archer Slack app is installed in your workspace.
- Your workspace is linked to an Archer account.
- You know the recipient's email address.

## Place an order

<Steps>
  <Step title="Run the command">
    In any Slack channel or DM, type `/order` and press **Enter**. The **Place an Order** modal opens.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the item">
    In the **Item Name** field, type the name of the item you want to send.

    **Example:** `Yeti Tumbler`
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the recipient">
    In the **Recipient Email** field, enter the email address of the person who should receive the item.

    **Example:** `alice@example.com`
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit">
    Click **Submit**. Archer queues the order and replaces the modal with a confirmation message:

    ```text
    ✅ Got it! We'll send Yeti Tumbler to alice@example.com.
    ```

    Click **Cancel** at any time to close the modal without submitting.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What gets recorded

When you submit the modal, Archer records the order with:

- The item name you entered.
- The recipient's email address.
- The Slack user ID of the submitter.
- The Slack workspace the order was placed from.
- A UTC timestamp of when the order was received.

Both fields are required. If either is left blank, Slack shows a validation error and the order is not submitted.

<Note>
  `/order` is separate from `/refer`. Use `/order` to send a physical or digital item to a teammate, and `/refer` to refer a contact into your Archer workflows.
</Note>

<Tip>
  Need to send the same item to several people? Run `/order` once per recipient — each submission is recorded as its own order.
</Tip>
